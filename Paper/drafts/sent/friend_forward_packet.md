>
> It starts from a topology problem in conventional Dyson swarms: at a common heliocentric radius, Keplerian orbital planes inevitably intersect, so a dense same-shell swarm accumulates persistent nodal crossing structure. At the other extreme, fully radiatively supported statite or bubble concepts avoid those crossings, but only at very demanding areal-density thresholds.
>
> The manuscript argues that these should be understood as parts of a continuous support-and-stratification spectrum. Using established solar-sail displaced non-Keplerian orbit (DNKO) theory, it develops one low-latitude mixed-support branch within that spectrum, called a Micro-Displaced Dyson Swarm (MDDS). In this regime, collectors remain primarily orbital while modest solar-radiation-pressure support maintains small out-of-plane offsets and creates stratified latitude bands. For the ideal specular case, this yields a closed-form support relation and an equivalent areal-density screening criterion for layered swarm design.
>
