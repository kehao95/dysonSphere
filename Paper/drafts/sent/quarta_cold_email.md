## Short Abstract

Conventional Dyson swarms inherit a topology problem because same-shell Keplerian orbital planes generically intersect at shared nodal corridors, whereas fully radiatively supported bubble/statite concepts avoid that geometry only at severe areal-density cost. I therefore argue that Dyson architectures are better understood as a continuous support spectrum between purely orbital swarms and purely radiatively supported bubbles. Building on displaced non-Keplerian orbit theory, the manuscript makes that continuum explicit and then develops one concrete low-beta displaced branch, termed a Micro-Displaced Dyson Swarm (MDDS), in which collectors remain primarily orbital while solar-radiation pressure maintains small out-of-plane displacements that create stratified latitude bands. For an ideal specular sail, this yields a closed-form support relation and an equivalent areal-density screening criterion. The manuscript is framed not as discovering the displaced-orbit bridge itself, but as developing it into a continuous Dyson support spectrum and an analytic architecture criterion for layered swarm design.
