## Strategic Conclusion

The current manuscript should be prepared first for *Acta Astronautica*. Its strongest position is not categorical orbit-family novelty. The strongest position is an analytic and architectural extension: a low-latitude displaced non-Keplerian support branch is developed into a screening relation for stratified Dyson-swarm architectures.

The controlling one-sentence framing is:

> A reduced low-latitude solar-sail support model gives a closed-form screening curve for radiation-pressure-assisted stratification of Dyson swarm architectures.

This implies an Acta-style paper rather than a pure celestial-mechanics paper or an astronomy/technosignature paper. The manuscript should read as an astrodynamics / space-systems architecture / solar-sail-enabled mission-concept paper with clear model boundaries.

## Acta Positioning Rules

Use this framing:

- The work extends low-latitude displaced non-Keplerian support analysis into an architecture-level screening model.
- The result links displacement latitude, lightness number, and system areal density.
- Dyson-swarm language is used as systems architecture context, not as a near-term feasibility claim.
- The novelty is architectural and screening-oriented rather than orbital-family discovery.

Avoid these claims:

- Do not claim a new displaced-orbit family.
- Do not claim MDDS is fully stable.
- Do not claim PV fill factor closes an optical-power architecture.
- Do not claim the model solves Dyson-swarm collisions.
- Do not treat the low-latitude formula as a full high-latitude ideal-sail branch.
