## Accepted Prior-Art Map

### 1. Foundational Dyson Literature

Core role in manuscript:

- Establish the origin of artificial stellar energy collection and infrared waste-heat framing.
- Set up why a literal rigid shell is not the active target of the present paper.
- Position MDDS inside the swarm / bubble / statite taxonomy rather than outside it.

Current canonical references:

- Dyson (1960), `dyson1960infrared`
- Wright et al. (2015), `wright2015gies`
- Wright (2020), `wright2020dysonspheres`

Manuscript implication:

The paper should continue to frame MDDS as an architecture-language contribution inside the Dyson literature, not as a claim that Dyson structures themselves are newly motivated.

### 2. Statites, Solar Sails, and Displaced Non-Keplerian Orbits

Core role in manuscript:

- Establish that the dynamical substrate is already mature.
- Ground the use of lightness number, sail pitch, and displaced circular-orbit balance in prior solar-sail theory.
- Prevent overclaiming the orbit-family aspect of MDDS.

Current canonical references:

- Forward (1991), `forward1991statite`
- McInnes and Brown (1990), `mcinnesbrown1990nonpoint`
- McInnes and Simmons (1992a,b), `mcinnes1992haloorbits1`, `mcinnes1992haloorbits2`
- McInnes (1997), `mcinnes1997displaced`
- McInnes (1998), `mcinnes1998dnko`
- McInnes (1999), `mcinnes1999solarsailing`
- Simo and McInnes (2010), `simo2010displaced`
- Wawrzyniak and Howell (2011), `wawrzyniak2011generating`

Manuscript implication:

The safest wording remains: the present paper develops a known displaced-orbit toolkit into a Dyson architecture screening framework.

### 3. Synchronous Displaced-Orbit Branches

Core role in manuscript:

- Show that Earth-/planet-synchronous displaced branches are already known operational variants.
- Support the manuscript's interpretation that synchronization slices the same support space rather than changing the underlying support curve.

Current canonical references:

- Heiligers and McInnes (2015), `heiligers2015earthfollowing`
- Quarta, Mengali, and Bassetto (2020), `quarta2020earthsync`
- Bassetto and Quarta (2024), `bassetto2024marssync`

Manuscript implication:

Do not present Earth-synchronous MDDS as a new orbit family. Present it as an illustrative operational constraint applied to the support framework.

### 4. Large-Constellation Collision and Reconfiguration Literature

Core role in manuscript:

- Provide modern support for the claim that dense populations create management burdens, without claiming a fully quantified Dyson-scale traffic model.
- Keep the collision/topology motivation credible but bounded.

Current canonical references:

- Walker (1971), `walker1971patterns`
- Radtke et al. (2017), `radtke2017oneweb`
- Le May et al. (2018), `lemay2018collision`
- de Weck et al. (2004), `deweck2004staged`
- Lee et al. (2018), `lee2018staged`
- Chen et al. (2024), `chen2024shortestpath`
- Lee et al. (2025), `lee2025mcrp`

Manuscript implication:

The present paper may motivate future traffic metrics such as node-intersection count, minimum normal separation, conjunction-corridor density, and reconfiguration burden. It should not yet claim an operational collision-avoidance solution.

### 5. McInnes 2025-2026 Novelty Boundary

Core role in manuscript:

- McInnes (2025) supports the broader recent shift toward revisiting the stability of idealized megastructures.
- McInnes (2026) is the key novelty constraint: it already points toward using displaced non-Keplerian orbit families / parallel stacking to reduce Dyson-swarm collision structure.

Current canonical references:

- McInnes (2025), `mcinnes2025ringworlds`
- McInnes (2026), `mcinnes2026bubbles`

Manuscript implication:

The paper should not claim discovery of the displaced-NKO bridge. The claim should remain bridge development: support continuum, analytic screening criterion, and staged-growth architecture language.

## Accepted Novelty Boundary

The current manuscript's strongest claim is:

> The novelty is not that displaced non-Keplerian orbits can exist, nor that they can in principle relieve Dyson-swarm intersections. The novelty is the development of that known bridge into a continuous Dyson support spectrum with explicit screening variables, a low-latitude analytic branch, and a staged-growth architecture language.

This is consistent with the existing `Paper/README.md` posture and should remain the controlling formulation.

## Rejected or Deferred From DR Output

The `dr` reports suggested several stronger directions that should not be imported into the current manuscript:

- Do not claim a decentralized smart-dust or subsumption-control architecture.
- Do not claim MDDS has "high stability" as an architecture; the current result is only local boundedness in the branch-specific `r-\phi` subsystem plus a neutral along-track mode.
- Do not claim parallel stacking prevents collisional cascades under failure. The current paper only establishes geometry and screening-level supportability.
- Do not shift the current manuscript into a full continuum-mechanics or fluid-dynamic swarm model.
- Do not add complex Earth-Moon-Mars relay architecture claims without a separate design study.

These may be future research directions, but importing them now would blur the paper's bounded framework contribution.

## Gaps To Carry Forward

These are legitimate future-work gaps after the current manuscript:

- Passive and closed-loop stability closure beyond the branch-specific local `r-\phi` slice.
- Finite-source and finite-extent force-law corrections.
- Optical non-idealities, payload-coupled force vectors, and thrust-direction errors.
- Distributed loading, in-plane stress, tether/boom mass, and fixed bus overhead.
- Mutual attenuation, self-shadowing, diffuse reradiation, and secular optical drift.
- Quantified topology / traffic metrics for staged Dyson-swarm growth.
- Observational or technosignature modeling for flattened, stratified developing systems.
